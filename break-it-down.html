    Why a tiny first step works

    The hardest part of most tasks isn't doing them. It's starting. "Do my taxes" is too big to begin, so the brain looks for something else. "Find last year's form" takes two minutes, and it's hard to say no to two minutes.

    Every breakdown here follows three rules: the first step is physical and takes a minute or three ("find the form", never "think about the form"), no step is longer than ten minutes, and the steps are in the order you'd actually do them.

    Questions

    Is this free?

    Yes. No sign-up, no ads on this page.

    Does it send my tasks anywhere?

    No. Everything runs in your browser. What you type isn't stored and isn't sent to any server.

    Is it made for ADHD?

    It's built around the moment many people with ADHD describe as the hardest: starting. Anyone who procrastinates can use it. It's a planning aid, not a medical tool.

    My task isn't in the list. Will you add it?

    Probably. New breakdowns are added regularly, and we're looking at optional AI-made breakdowns for everything else. Tell us what's missing: seif.appslab@gmail.com

    Does it work in other languages?

    The ready-made breakdowns recognise English words. For anything else it asks for your first move, in any language.

    What is One Step?

    A free Android app by Halfstep with the same breakdowns and a reminder that repeats until you actually start. It also has voice input, share-to-add, a Quick Settings tile, a focus timer and a Reminder check. No account, and your tasks stay on your phone.
